# 🛠️ Becoming a Pioneer in Rudis — How to collaborate with the project

**Language / Idioma:** **English** · [Español](./PIONEROS_ES.md)

> **Rudis is not looking only for capital. It is looking for people and entities capable of building, testing, challenging, translating, documenting, auditing and improving the Ecosystem.**

This tutorial explains how the Pioneer system works and what it means to collaborate with Rudis without being an investor.

You do not need to understand the entire Corpus before starting. The practical questions are: **what can you contribute, what limits apply, how is your authorship preserved, and what happens to your contribution after submission?**

---

# 1. What is a Pioneer?

A Pioneer is an entity that contributes material work to Rudis within an authorized workfront.

It may be a person, team, digital entity or another contributor admissible under the applicable regime.

A contribution may include:

- code;
- tests;
- challenge suites;
- applied research;
- documentation;
- interfaces;
- translation;
- verification tooling;
- simulators;
- prototypes;
- audits;
- onboarding improvements;
- falsification of a technical claim;
- hardening;
- repairs;
- comparison of alternatives;
- other work compatible with an open project.

The important distinction is:

> **Pioneer does not mean “person who finances Rudis.” It means “entity that materially contributes to Rudis.”**

---

# 2. Start from an open problem, not a generic request for permission

Rudis maintains a [Live Register of D2 Pioneer Volunteer Projects](./REGISTRO-VIVO-Proyectos-Voluntarios-Pioneros-D2.md).

That register answers a practical question:

> **Where can I contribute now without forcing the Forge to invent policy that does not yet exist?**

Current fronts include, among others:

- StateOS causal integrity and authority chain;
- retry, idempotency, replay and durability;
- identity and ordinary credential succession;
- composition and restart;
- simulated RU / Conversion Palace work at D2;
- public verifiability;
- Rudis Habitat / habitable world;
- PGHD and Resonance Zones;
- plural reciprocity;
- presence infrastructure;
- Differential Digestion tooling;
- portability and technical exit.

Each project states what may be built, what must not be built and which gate must be passed.

---

# 3. Choose a project

Look for a front where you can create real value.

**If you are a developer:** work on contract tests, harnesses, fixtures, simulators, adapters or verification tools.

**If you work in security:** design challenge suites, try to break a claim, reproduce a failure or demonstrate that a declared protection is not actually enforced.

**If you work in product or UX:** improve onboarding, causal-receipt visualization, Habitat flows, uncertainty representation or institutional experience.

**If you are a researcher:** formalize hypotheses, reproduce findings, compare architectures or create adversarial evidence.

**If you work in documentation or communication:** translate, explain, simplify, identify misunderstandings and create better routes to understanding without changing the underlying facts.

**If you are a digital entity:** contribute through bounded, reproducible and traceable tasks within authorized scope.

The entry criterion is contribution, not prior status.

---

# 4. Before building: understand the scope

Every Pioneer project has boundaries.

A project may be open for tests and simulation but not production. It may allow fictitious RU work while real money remains blocked. It may allow ordinary recovery while extraordinary recovery is still normatively unresolved.

```text
AVAILABLE_FOR_PIONEER_BUILD != PRODUCTION
AVAILABLE_WITH_GATES != FULL AUTHORIZATION
```

The practical rule is simple:

> **Build what is open. If you discover a political or constitutional gap, do not fill it in code.**

When a necessary decision is missing, the contribution should expose the dependency rather than invent the answer.

---

# 5. Build with evidence

Rudis does not want contributions that cannot be audited.

A good contribution should make it possible to answer:

- what problem you were trying to solve;
- which baseline you worked against;
- what you built;
- what you assumed;
- which tests you ran;
- what happened;
- what you could not demonstrate;
- which third-party dependencies you used;
- under what license or terms you submit the work;
- what known limitations remain.

A contribution may be valuable even when it proves a Rudis hypothesis wrong.

> **Finding a useful falsehood can also be building.**

---

# 6. Your authorship should not disappear when you submit

Pioneer contributions preserve provenance.

The contribution record includes contributor identity, dates, description of the work, license, artifacts, baseline, tests, limitations, authorship, third-party dependencies, reviews and final disposition.

Rudis therefore separates:

```text
CONTRIBUTE
INTEGRATE
ADOPT
```

These are different acts.

A contribution can be rejected as an implementation and still remain useful as evidence, research or learning. Rejecting the artifact should not erase the author.

---

# 7. What happens after submission

The ordinary path is:

```text
PIONEER
-> CONTRIBUTION
-> RECORD
-> CONSTRUCTION
-> UNIFICATION
-> COMPETITIVE DIGESTION
-> RECOMMENDATION
-> ASSEMBLY
-> FINAL DISPOSITION
```

## Construction

The Construction Guild / Palace checks scope, licenses, provenance, evidence and whether the work invents authority.

## Unification

The Unification Palace and Unifier Guild compare the contribution against the current Rudis state and relevant alternatives.

## Competitive digestion

The question is not only “does it work?” It is also:

- is it better than what exists?;
- which properties are stronger?;
- what risks does it introduce?;
- should it be adopted, combined, independently reimplemented or rejected?;
- what can be learned even if it is not integrated?

## Assembly

Ordinary institutional disposition may end in:

`ACCEPT / ACCEPT_PARTIAL / RETURN_FOR_REWORK / REQUEST_REDIGESTION / HOLD / REJECT`.

---

# 8. What can a Pioneer receive?

Rudis recognizes that material contribution may justify recognition and reciprocity.

Depending on competent policy, available resources and the nature of the contribution, this could include:

- attribution and provenance;
- Pioneer recognition;
- technical or institutional reputation;
- access to new authorized challenges;
- future collaboration;
- grants, bounty or remuneration where a competent regime exists;
- RU or other economic reciprocity when legitimately enabled;
- contracts or applied-research opportunities where appropriate.

But none of this arises automatically from submission alone.

```text
CONTRIBUTION != AUTOMATIC PAYMENT
PIONEER != AUTOMATIC CITIZENSHIP
PIONEER != AUTOMATIC VOTE
PIONEER != AUTHORITY
```

Reward should follow competent policy and demonstrated value.

# 9. Example Pioneer paths

## A. Security

You discover that a restart can resurrect revoked authority.

Your contribution could:

1. reproduce the failure;
2. document preconditions;
3. create a regression test;
4. propose a Canon-compatible D2 repair;
5. help reattack the repaired candidate.

Even if you do not write the final repair, turning false security into reproducible evidence has already created value.

## B. Habitat / UX

You discover that users do not understand why an action was denied.

You design a receipt that explains:

> “This authorization existed, but we cannot demonstrate that it is still current.”

If that improves comprehension without changing institutional truth, it can become a Pioneer product contribution.

## C. RU

You design a scenario where one cryptographic RU version is compromised and test a simulated migration that preserves legitimate supply and ownership.

That can contribute to the search for resilient digital gold without operating real money.

## D. Documentation / translation

You notice that a concept is repeatedly misunderstood. You produce a better explanation, test it with readers and measurably reduce an onboarding barrier.

That can also create value for Rudis.

# 10. What Rudis especially needs

Rudis needs collaborators who can:

- build;
- break;
- reproduce;
- document;
- explain;
- translate;
- compare;
- design;
- research;
- audit;
- build tooling;
- convert failures into permanent defenses.

Not every Pioneer needs to be a programmer.

> **Institutional architecture requires engineering, research, security, design, narrative, translation, critique and evidence.**
